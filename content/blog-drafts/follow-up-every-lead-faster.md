# How insurance agencies and real estate brokers can follow up with every lead faster (without new software)

**Short answer:** Write down every way a lead reaches you, then make sure each one does three things automatically: sends the person a quick, honest acknowledgement, alerts the right person on your team, and creates a reminder if nobody follows up. Most agencies and brokerages can do this in the CRM and phone system they already pay for. Keep a person in charge of the actual conversation.

## Why speed matters more than another tool

When someone asks for a quote or a showing, they're often asking more than one business at the same time. In a widely cited 2011 *Harvard Business Review* article, researchers sent test web inquiries to 2,241 U.S. companies. 37% responded within an hour, and 23% never responded at all. In a separate data set of 1.25 million sales leads, they found that firms trying to contact a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a meaningful conversation with a decision maker as firms that waited even an hour longer. ([HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads"](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads).)

The data is old and comes from a broad mix of industries, so treat it as a direction, not a forecast for your office. The point holds anyway: slow, inconsistent follow-up costs you conversations you already paid to get. The fix is usually a process, not a new platform.

## Step 1: List every way a lead reaches you

Before you automate anything, write down every entry point:

- Website quote or contact forms
- Phone calls, including the ones you miss
- Texts to your business number
- Listing inquiries from portals or your IDX search (brokerages)
- Carrier, comparison-site or referral leads (agencies)
- Facebook or Google lead forms, and walk-ins
- Your open-house sign-in sheet

For each one, note where it lands today (whose inbox, whose phone, which spreadsheet) and who is supposed to respond. The gaps usually show up right away: a form that emails someone who's on vacation, a portal inbox nobody checks on weekends, missed calls that just go to voicemail.

## Step 2: Send every lead into one place

Pick the system you already use as your CRM. For most of our builds that's GoHighLevel, but the idea is the same anywhere. Connect each entry point so a new lead creates or updates a contact there, with a few useful fields:

- Where the lead came from
- What they asked about (line of insurance, or the property address or listing)
- Their preferred way to be contacted, and whether they agreed to texts

If a source can't connect directly, a simple forwarding rule or form integration is usually enough. You're aiming for one list everyone trusts.

## Step 3: Acknowledge right away, honestly

Set up an automatic text and/or email that goes out as soon as the lead comes in. Keep it short and true:

> "Hi {first name}, thanks for reaching out to {Agency/Brokerage}. We got your request about {topic}. {Name} will contact you {realistic time frame, e.g. 'today' or 'by tomorrow morning'}. Reply here if there's a better time to reach you."

A few rules:

- **Don't pretend to be a person** if a person isn't typing. Plain and friendly works better than fake chat.
- **Don't quote prices, coverage or availability** in the automatic message. That's a licensed or human conversation.
- **Only text people who agreed to it.** Texting rules for businesses are strict. Confirm your consent wording and opt-out handling with your compliance contact. The FCC's consumer guide is a useful starting point: [Stop Unwanted Robocalls and Texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts).

## Step 4: Turn on missed-call text back

Missed calls are often your warmest leads, and often the easiest to fix. Most phone and CRM systems, including GoHighLevel, can send an automatic text when a call to your business line goes unanswered:

> "Sorry we missed your call at {Agency/Brokerage}. Is this about a quote, a policy, or a property? Reply here and we'll get right back to you."

That reply comes back into the same conversation in your CRM, so nobody has to dig through a phone log.

## Step 5: Route it to the right person

Decide on simple routing rules and write them down:

- **Agencies:** by line of insurance (auto/home vs. commercial vs. life), by language, or by producer for existing customers.
- **Brokerages:** by listing agent, by area or by round robin among agents on duty.

When a lead is assigned, that person gets an instant notification (app, text or email) with the lead's details and a one-tap way to call or reply. Fair distribution matters, but don't let a perfect rotation slow down the first response.

## Step 6: Add reminders and a backup

Automations should help people follow up, not replace them. Add:

- A reminder to the assigned person if the lead hasn't been contacted within your target window
- An alert to a manager or backup person if it's still untouched after that
- A short follow-up sequence (for example, a check-in text the next day and an email a few days later) that **stops automatically** once the lead replies or a team member marks it contacted

## Step 7: Keep a human in the loop

Before anything goes live, have someone on your team review every automatic message: tone, accuracy, required disclosures and opt-out language. Then test it yourself. Submit your own form, call and hang up, text your business number, and check what arrives and who gets notified.

Once a month, look at a handful of recent leads:

- How long did the first real (human) response take?
- Did anyone fall through the cracks?
- Did any automatic message confuse people?

Adjust the wording and rules based on what you see. Don't just add more messages.

## What you probably don't need

You likely don't need a new CRM, a chatbot or another subscription. Most of what's above can be built in tools agencies and brokerages already have, especially GoHighLevel. The work is mapping your lead sources, writing honest messages and setting clear ownership. That's the "maximize what you have" approach: use the tools you already pay for, save your team time and stop leads from slipping away.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] Every lead source listed, with where it lands today
- [ ] All sources feeding one CRM list
- [ ] Instant acknowledgement (text/email) with honest timing
- [ ] Text consent and opt-out confirmed with your compliance contact
- [ ] Missed-call text back turned on
- [ ] Written routing rules and instant notifications
- [ ] Reminders plus a backup/escalation person
- [ ] Follow-up sequence stops when the lead replies
- [ ] Every message reviewed by a person and tested end to end
- [ ] Monthly look at a handful of real leads
