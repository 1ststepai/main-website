**Short answer:** Hiring someone to build GoHighLevel workflows can cost anywhere from a few hundred dollars for a single simple workflow to several thousand for multi-step workflows with integrations. Price depends on how many steps, tools and people are involved, not on the software. At 1stStep.ai, automations start at $2,500 per workflow, scoped on a free 30-minute call. ## What public pricing looks like

A public search in November 2026 showed GoHighLevel freelancers and agencies quoting a wide range: basic single-workflow setups from a few hundred dollars, fuller builds with funnels and integrations around one to three thousand, and complex custom API or webhook work above that. Hourly rates also vary widely. Treat these as a map of the market, not a quote, and compare what's included.

## What drives the price

- **Number of entry points.** One web form is simpler than forms, phones, portals and carrier leads together.
- **Routing rules.** Sending every lead to one person is simple; routing by line of insurance, territory or agent availability takes more work and testing.
- **Integrations.** Connecting GoHighLevel to an outside system (an agency management system, IDX, a quoting tool) adds time.
- **Texting setup.** Business texting in the US needs A2P 10DLC registration and consent handling, which is part of doing it properly.
- **Testing and handoff.** Testing every path end to end and documenting what was built is part of a real build.

## What to build first

If you already pay for GoHighLevel, start with what protects the leads you already get:

1. **Instant acknowledgement.** When a form or lead source comes in, send a short, honest text or email confirming you got it and when a person will reach out.
2. **Missed-call text back.** When a call goes unanswered, send a text from the same number so the caller isn't left at voicemail. Only text people where you have a lawful basis and include opt-out language.
3. **No-contact reminder.** If nobody has contacted the lead within your target time, alert the assigned person and a backup.
4. **Simple routing.** Send each lead to the right person based on a rule your team can explain.
5. **A short follow-up sequence that stops on reply.** A few touches over the first couple of weeks, ending automatically when the lead responds or books.

## Things to avoid

- **Automating a broken process.** If nobody agrees who owns a lead, automation just moves the confusion faster. That's what a [bottleneck audit](/blog/what-is-a-bottleneck-audit/) is for.
- **Pretending a bot is a person.** Keep automated messages plain and honest.
- **Quoting prices or coverage automatically.** Keep licensed or pricing conversations with a person.
- **Buying more software first.** Most of the above can be built in GoHighLevel and the phone system you already have.
