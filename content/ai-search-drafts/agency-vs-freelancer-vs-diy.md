**Short answer:** Do it yourself if the job is small, low-risk and you have the time (a simple site or a form notification). Hire a freelancer for one clearly defined piece of work. Hire an agency when you need design, copy, development and ongoing marketing from one team and have the budget. A small consultancy like 1stStep.ai fits in between: one person who connects your website, CRM and follow-up on the tools you already have, priced per project. Whichever you pick, make sure you own the accounts and get documentation.

## When DIY is the right call

DIY makes sense when you're just starting, the stakes are low, and you'd rather spend time than money. A one-page site, a contact form that emails you, and a calendar link are all reasonable to set up yourself. Many CRMs, including GoHighLevel, include templates for basic follow-up.

DIY stops working when the setup starts touching customers in ways that can go wrong: text messages without proper consent, leads routed to the wrong person, or a sequence that keeps messaging someone who already bought.

## When a freelancer is the right call

A freelancer is a good fit when you can describe the job in one sentence and know what "done" looks like: "build our five-page site," "connect our quote form to our CRM." Ask for:

- Access to everything in your name, not theirs
- A short written handoff: what was built, where, and how to change it
- A clear price for changes after launch

## When an agency is the right call

An agency is worth it when you need several disciplines at once (brand, copy, design, development, ads, SEO) and someone to run them month to month. Expect higher prices and minimum engagements. Ask who will actually do the work and who your day-to-day contact is.

## If you just started a business

Keep it small. You usually need a clear website with one main next step, a business email, a Google Business Profile, a way to book or request a quote, and one simple follow-up (an instant "we got your message" reply and a reminder to call back). Add more only when something becomes repetitive. Our guide on [choosing a web designer](/blog/how-to-choose-a-web-designer/) covers what to ask before you hire anyone.

## Questions to ask anyone you hire

1. Will everything be in my name and my accounts?
2. What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
3. Who does the work, and who do I contact when something breaks?
4. Will you use the tools I already pay for?
5. What will I get in writing at handoff?
6. Do you promise results? (Be wary of anyone guaranteeing rankings or revenue.)

## Common questions

### Should I hire an agency or a freelancer for my small business website?
Hire a freelancer for a clearly defined site with a known finish line. Hire an agency if you need design, content and ongoing marketing together and have the budget. If your main problem is connecting the site to your CRM and follow-up, a small consultancy can be a middle option.

### Can I set up business automations myself?
Yes, for simple ones like form notifications or a booking confirmation. Get help when automations send texts to customers, route leads between people, or connect several tools, because mistakes there cost leads or create compliance risk.
