How to Start Using AI in Your Small Business: Your First Task, Prompt and Review

By Evan Pancis · 1stStep.ai · November 11, 2026

Short answer: Start with one low-risk task you already understand. Supply public or fictional facts, ask for a draft, check it, then decide whether to use it. You don't need to code.

Examples in this article are fictional and written for teaching.

Which everyday business tasks can AI help with first?

Choose a draft you can check yourself before anyone else sees it. Keep automatic sending, sensitive customer data and legal or tax advice for later, with appropriate help and approval.

Three manageable first tasks
Try firstGive itCheck yourself
Draft a customer email replyA fictional question and your approved public factsDoes it answer the question without promising anything extra?
Summarize public notesPublished notes you understandDid it miss a condition or change the meaning?
Draft FAQ answersPublished hours and servicesAre all details supported by your fact sheet?

Do I need to know coding?

No. You can type instructions into an AI assistant in everyday language. Asking for a draft is different from building software. For this practice, you only need a task you understand and the ability to check the answer.

Optional: keep your practice files in one simple folder

A folder can help you keep the facts, draft and review together. Use short notes like these:

business-ai-practice/
├── 01-context/
│   ├── skill-prepare-context.txt
│   └── business-facts.txt
├── 02-draft/
│   ├── skill-draft-faq.txt
│   └── faq-draft.txt
└── 03-review/
    ├── skill-check-answer.txt
    └── reviewed-draft.txt

Here, “skill” just means reusable written instructions you paste in yourself. For example, your review instructions might say, “Compare each claim with my fact sheet and flag anything missing.” These folders do not auto-load into any AI tool. One document works just as well.

Worked example: drafting FAQ answers for a fictional business

This is an illustrative example written for this article, not a recorded tool run. The business, prompts, draft and mistake below are fictional teaching material.

Fictional fact sheet

  • Business: Maple Street Dog Grooming, Morris County, NJ — fictional.
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; appointments required.
  • Services: dog bathing, brushing and nail trims.
  • Not known: prices, cancellation fees, cancellation policy or refund policy.

What does a weak prompt leave out?

Write FAQs for my dog grooming business.

That leaves the assistant guessing about services, hours and policies. Give it the boundaries instead.

What does an improved prompt include?

Act as a writing assistant drafting FAQs for dog owners considering Maple Street Dog Grooming. Use only the fictional fact sheet below: Morris County, NJ; Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; appointments required; dog bathing, brushing and nail trims. Prices, cancellation fees, cancellation policy and refund policy are not known.

Write three Q&As, each under 60 words, answering: When are you open? What services do you offer? What is your cancellation policy? Mark anything not in the facts “Needs confirmation.” Do not invent prices, policies or promises. Use a friendly, plain-English tone.

What might the draft look like?

Illustrative draft — not approved for use

When are you open?
Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Appointments are required.

What services do you offer?
We offer dog bathing, brushing and nail trims. Satisfaction guaranteed.

What is your cancellation policy?
Needs confirmation: the fact sheet does not include a cancellation policy or cancellation fee.

Human review: The draft correctly flags the missing cancellation policy, but “Satisfaction guaranteed” has no support in the facts. Remove that sentence. Keep the cancellation answer marked “Needs confirmation” in your working document until the owner supplies a written policy; do not publish it as a policy.

A clearer prompt helps set boundaries. It does not replace checking the output.

How do I check the AI's draft?

Compare the draft with your facts, line by line. Use this checklist each time:

  • Each claim matches the facts I supplied.
  • Prices, dates and policies are verified against my current records.
  • I have not pasted anything private.
  • The tone sounds like me and fits the reader.
  • A person decides what to use, edit or reject.
  • Nothing is sent automatically.

What should I try next?

Try a reply to a fictional opening-hours question, a summary of a public event announcement or another FAQ based on published services. Keep the same review habit. Before using real business details, follow the guide to what to keep private and how to check AI answers.

Frequently asked questions

Which task should I start with?

Start with a small draft you already know how to check: a fictional customer email reply, a summary of public notes or FAQs based on published business facts. Keep it private until you have reviewed it.

What facts should I give the AI?

Give only the public or fictional details needed for the task, such as published hours and services. Say what is unknown, who the draft is for and what format you want. Ask it to flag missing details rather than guess.

Can I use a free AI tool to practice?

Often yes for public or fictional practice. Check the current plan’s limits and data settings before you begin, and follow any company rules. You can practice writing and reviewing a prompt without sharing real customer information.
